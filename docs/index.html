The decision in one screen

Following a swimmer with a motorized stage means closing a control loop around a plant that includes the water. Every stage acceleration sloshes the chamber, the organism rides the slosh, and the controller chases its own disturbance on top of mechanical ringing. Doable, but it becomes the whole project.

Instead: run low magnification on a global-shutter sensor, detect and track every organism in software, and crop a follow-window around the one you care about. Zero lag, multiple targets at once, and the predator entering frame is free. The stage stays manual. The only servo loop is Z focus, which is slow, low-mass, has no fluid coupling, and uses image sharpness as a clean error signal.

Contrast beats magnification. Pond protists are transparent; darkfield (a $20 condenser stop) makes them glow on black at 4x–10x. Phase contrast is a 10x-and-up feature and costs $500+ more. Skipped for v1.

Tracker v1 on synthetic swimmers

What the tracker is being tested against before there is a culture to film. synth.py renders seeded swimmers with a ground-truth CSV; track.py runs MOG2 background subtraction, morphology, contours, a constant-velocity Kalman filter per track and greedy nearest-neighbour assignment, then writes the overlay, the CSV and a follow crop. Six swimmers, 640×480, 300 frames at 30 fps.

Scale caveat. At the planned 4x setup (1.5 µm/px) these bodies would be ~18 µm long moving at ~135 µm/s. A Paramecium is 10× longer and 7× faster, 11 px per frame at 60 fps. The defaults still cover that, but nothing here proves it. Real footage will.

Design

Architecture signal path

The numbers that make this work (AR0234 sensor, 3 µm pixels, 1920 px wide, 0.5x reducer):

  • 4x objective → 1.5 µm/px, field 2.9 × 1.8 mm. Paramecium at 1 mm/s moves 17 µm = 11 px per frame at 60 fps. Trivial to track; a Didinium strike fits in frame.
  • 10x objective → 0.6 µm/px, field 1.15 × 0.7 mm. Same swimmer is 28 px/frame. You lose it off the edge in about a second, so this is the follow-crop mode.
  • Motion blur: 1 mm/s × 2 ms exposure = 2 µm, about a pixel at 4x. Exposure stays ≤2 ms, which means a bright LED through the stop, not the stock bulb.
  • Depth of field at 4x is ~50 µm; at 10x ~10 µm. In a 150 µm chamber the autofocus loop does real work at 10x and almost none at 4x.
Keep the Pi dumb: capture, detect, track, clip, focus. Everything that needs a model or a second look runs on the Mac Studio over the network. That split keeps the realtime loop deterministic.
Software plan v1 → v3, then 3D

v1, Pi only. picamera2 at 1920×1200, 60 fps, 2 ms exposure, frames to a ring buffer. MOG2 background subtraction (darkfield makes this nearly binary), contour → centroid + area + aspect, one Kalman filter per track, assignment. Output: full-frame H.264 plus a per-track CSV and a cropped follow clip of any track that lives longer than a couple of seconds. Detect on downscaled frames, cut crops from the full-res buffer.

v2, autofocus. Variance of Laplacian on the current follow crop as the error signal; Z stepper via TMC2209 UART; PID with a deadband wider than depth of field so it doesn't hunt. Hill-climb on startup, then hold. Rate-limited to ~2 µm/s so the image never visibly breathes.

v3, meaning. Events from geometry alone: two tracks converge and one disappears is a capture; a sessile blob's area drops 80 % in one frame is a Stentor contraction. Events trigger a 10 s pre-roll clip. Subtract the per-frame median motion of all tracks before calling anything "swimming", because the LED's convection reads as drift. Then a small YOLO fine-tune on our own crops for species labels, offline on the Mac Studio, and an ecology dashboard: who eats whom, encounter rates, speed distributions.

3D, and why tilt won't do it. Depth of field is 10–50 µm, the chamber is 150 µm, and a microscope image has no perspective, so a tilted view is the same silhouette, blurrier. The routes that work: focal sweep (ramp Z at 10–20 Hz while recording; sharpness per track per frame gives Z; free, uses the motor we already need) and lensless digital holography (bare sensor, point LED, no objective; software refocuses any plane after the fact and gives true 3D tracks of every swimmer at once; ~$60; the reconstruction is a Fresnel propagation). Light-field and stereo rejected.

Sample prep the part that decides everything
  1. Collect from still water with plant matter: squeeze pond weed over a jar, scrape the underside of lily pads, take the surface film and a little sediment. Bring a second jar of plain pond water for dilution.
  2. Enrich at home: two-thirds full, lid loose, one boiled rice or wheat grain, windowsill. Bacteria bloom days 1–3 (cloudy, sulfur smell), Paramecium days 5–10 (a glittering cloud under side light), predators days 10–20. Milky white means overfed: pour half, top up with pond water.
  3. Concentrate for filming: let the jar sit, then pipette from the top (aerobes) or the bottom film (grazers).
  4. Chamber: well slide for 1 mm organisms. For the 100–300 µm ciliates, two strips of 100 µm spacer tape on a plain slide, coverslip bridged over them, edges sealed with a vaseline bead. Quasi-2D, no squash, no evaporation for an hour.
  5. Light: LED only. Halogen cooks samples and the heat convects, which looks like current to the tracker.
  6. Slowing agents (methylcellulose, Protoslo): skipped. They change behaviour and make every clip look like slow motion.
The chamber is the schedule risk, not the code. Expect a few evenings of dead Stentors and evaporated wells before there is a recipe that keeps a 1 mm organism alive in plane for an hour.

Risks and open questions
  • Used-scope lottery. Fungus in the optics, a sloppy fine focus, a phototube that isn't parfocal. Buy from a seller who shows a through-the-eyepiece photo; budget one return.
  • Darkfield at 10x needs a tighter stop and much more light. If it's dim, oblique illumination is the cheap fallback and looks almost like DIC.
  • Convection from the LED reads as drift in every track. Median-motion subtraction before any behaviour call.
  • MOG2 forgets slow swimmers. A resting Paramecium or a sessile rotifer vanishes from detection after ~--history frames. Fine for G1; v3 decides between a static-blob detector and a long-history second subtractor.
  • ID swaps on crossings. Greedy assignment is the known weakness; Hungarian assignment is a ten-line change if scipy becomes acceptable on the Pi.
  • Pi 5 throughput past 100 fps: crop at the sensor (ROI mode), not a faster computer.
  • Open: upright vs inverted. Inverted scopes film a petri dish with a real ecosystem in it; they're $3k+ new and rare used. v1 is upright.
End game a camera trap, not a camera

The rig runs unattended on a culture jar for days, the event detector tags captures, contractions and chases, and you come back to a folder of pre-rolled 10 s clips with a track overlay. The narrative work is choosing and cutting. A week of unattended running on a healthy culture should yield more predator-prey events than a month of hand-hunting.

The one place an XY stage comes back is event mode: when a trigger fires on a target near the frame edge, a single slow open-loop stage nudge re-centres it, then the stage stops. The slosh settles before the event finishes. v3, not before.

Path: darkfield tracker first, focal-sweep 3D second (free), holographic rig as a side experiment once the first clips are in the can. All three share the Pi, the LED driver and the chamber work.
Rejected alternatives do not relitigate without new evidence
  • Motorized XY tracking stage. Chases its own slosh. Digital follow-crop instead.
  • Phase contrast. $500+ and 10x-and-up. Darkfield stop instead.
  • PlanktoScope. A flow-through census imager; nothing hunts anything. Possibly a v4 species-count layer.
  • OpenFlexure v7. The open motorized-stage alternative; ~0.8 mm field in the low-cost build.
  • Tilt/roll for 3D, light-field, stereo. No depth at these scales, or not hobby parts, or hopeless for ciliates.
