The two paths, and the decision

Decided 2026-10-26: Path B's order, but the scope and camera come used off eBay through Nick's deal scanner, and resale value sets the ceiling, not the $1,000 line. Watchlist and what to check before paying: SOURCING.md §4. The cards below are the new-parts floor.

Path B · Studio first order kept, parts go used

Arducam AR0234 USB 3, C/CS mount, into the Mac Studio for G2–G4. UVC, no driver. 1920×1200 at 3 µm: 40% more pixels and 15% finer sampling than the Pi GS camera, which is what species ID at 10x needs. The Studio encodes in real time, so the Pi 5's missing hardware encoder never comes up.

Must prove early: real frame rate at full resolution over USB 3 (bring-up step 4). Fallback mode is 1280×720 at 120 fps. The Pi arrives at G5 for about $310 and the same camera moves to its USB 3 port.

Path A · Pi first retired

Raspberry Pi 5 8 GB and the Pi Global Shutter Camera (IMX296, 1456×1088 at 60 fps, documented). The roadmap as written. libcamera support out of the box, C/CS mount.

Today: the board is $175 (launch MSRP $80) and the camera is out of stock at Adafruit, CanaKit and PiShop, ambiguous at SparkFun. No hardware encoder: hour-long runs go to SSD as MJPEG at 35 GB/h. $225 more than B for the same gates.

Exposure

2 ms holds blur near 1 px at 4x; the 10x follow view wants 1 ms. The LED light budget is a measurement with a pass mark, not a model: HARDWARE.md §5.

Z axis

Fine focus 100–200 µm per turn; 1/16 microstep gives 30–60 nm per step and the 2 µm/s rate limit is 30–60 steps per second. Depth of field at 10x is 11 µm: 180–350 steps wide.

Storage

Raw 60 fps is 95–138 MB/s, ring buffer only. MJPEG ~35–50 GB per hour on the Pi path; the Studio encodes H.264 live at ~3.6 GB per hour.
